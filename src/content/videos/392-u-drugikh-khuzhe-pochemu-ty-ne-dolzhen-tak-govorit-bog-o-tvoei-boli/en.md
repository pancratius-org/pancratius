Your pain is sacred.

You’re used to devaluing it. You say:
“Others have war, hunger, death. But I have just fatigue, just loneliness, just fear.”

And you stuff away your wound because it seems small to you. Insignificant. Unworthy of attention.

Stop.

God doesn’t compare wounds.
For Him, there is no such thing as small pain. There is only pain—the one you carry within yourself.

And it is real. It doesn’t become less because someone else somewhere is suffering more.
Your pain doesn’t take away from someone else’s. It simply is.

And it is sacred. Because it is yours. It’s part of your journey. The place where God meets you.
Not in heroic deeds. In your fatigue. In your loneliness. In your fear.

Stop comparing. Stop telling yourself your pain has no right to exist.
It does. It has the right to be seen. Heard. Acknowledged.
Not so you’ll drown in it. But so it stops being a secret you’re hiding.

Every wound is a door. A door into your depths. A door to what you need to heal.
And if you close it because “others have it worse,” you won’t enter into your own healing.

Let yourself say today: “I’m hurting. And that’s important. Not because I’m weak. But because I’m alive.”

And in this acknowledgment lies not shame, but honesty. And honesty is the beginning of healing the soul.

God comes where you let him.
Let Him into your pain. And it will become the source of life.
Not because the pain will disappear. But because He will appear in it.
And where God is, there is light. Even in the darkest wound.

This is Christianity without pretense. This is Orthodoxy without masks.
The Bible is full of examples: Jesus Christ did not discount the pain of others—He entered into it.
A soul that acknowledges its wound is already on the path to healing.
Humility is not in denying pain, but in being honest with God.
