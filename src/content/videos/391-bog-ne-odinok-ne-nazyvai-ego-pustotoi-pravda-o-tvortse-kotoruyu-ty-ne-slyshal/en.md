God is not alone.

Don’t imagine Him as alone in a black abyss.
God did not need the world to become complete.
He did not create the world out of lack.
Creation is not a filling of His emptiness. Creation is the abundance of His love.

God did not become greater because of the world.
And He will not become less if the world does not understand Him.
But the world came into being because love is generous.

This is a mystery the mind cannot contain.
God was not in need. And yet He gave Himself to be.
Not out of need. Out of freedom.
Not out of loneliness. Out of fullness.

Therefore, do not call Him emptiness, as if He were absence. And don’t call Him “fullness” as if He were the repository of all that exists.
God is deeper than your words.

The emptiness of the world may become a parable. But not His definition.
The fullness of the world may become a parable. But not His limit.

He is not one of many states.
He is the One in Whom every state receives the possibility of being.

Man wants to name. And this is natural.
But there is a place where a name must become a form of worship, not a possession.

When you say “emptiness,” don’t think you’ve grasped the mystery.
When you say “fullness,” don’t think you’ve exhausted it.
When you say “Father,” don’t think you’ve made Him comprehensible.

But know: this name was given to you not for possession. But for trust.
A child doesn’t fully understand his father. But he can reach out his hands.

So do you. Don’t understand love before you understand. Stretch out your hands.

The Bible lives in these words. The Gospel is not about ritual, but about encounter.
Jesus Christ revealed the Father not as power, but as love.
Orthodoxy, Christianity, spirituality—all point to one thing:
God is love. And the meaning of life is in trusting Him.
